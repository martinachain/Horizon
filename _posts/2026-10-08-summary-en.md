Margaret Hamilton, the pioneering software engineer who led the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory team that developed the Apollo Guidance Computer's onboard flight software, has died, as reported by MIT News. She is credited with coining the term "software engineering" and led more than 400 people working on Apollo's software. Hamilton's work was instrumental in landing Apollo astronauts on the Moon and returning them safely, and her coinage of "software engineering" helped establish software development as a rigorous engineering discipline. Her death marks the loss of a foundational figure whose legacy shapes how software is built and valued today. Hamilton directed the Software Engineering Division at the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory (later Draper Laboratory), leading both the Lunar Module and Command and Service Module onboard flight software teams. A now-famous 1969 photo shows her standing beside listings of the Apollo software her teams developed.

In the 1960s, software development was not yet recognized as a formal engineering discipline, and Hamilton's insistence on rigorous processes and error handling helped ensure mission reliability. Her team's work included developing software that could recover from unexpected errors during flight.

## [Bigwords.page turns any screen into a sign using URL fragments](https://bigwords.page/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Bigwords.page is a backend-free web app that turns any screen into a sign by encoding the display message directly in the URL fragment (the part after the #). Because browsers never send URL fragments to the server, the message stays entirely client-side with no storage or backend involved. This approach is significant because it enables anyone to display a message on a remote or kiosk-mode screen simply by navigating to a crafted URL, with no server, account, or data storage required. It lowers the barrier for use cases like panel countdown timers, digital signage, and remote tablet displays. All configuration parameters are documented so messages can be generated programmatically, and the app relies on client-side rendering only. Community members noted a Firefox-specific bug where scrollWidth includes trailing spaces that extend into the margin, potentially causing text to overflow even when it visually fits.

hackernews · SpeakingOfBrad · Oct 7, 15:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49994443)

**Background**: A URL fragment identifier is the part of a URL that comes after the hash symbol (#), and by web specification it is not sent to the server. This makes fragments useful for client-side-only state, such as highlighting a section of a page or, in this case, carrying an entire message. A backend-free web app performs all logic in the browser without server-side processing, and kiosk displays are locked-down screens often used for signage or public information.
