## [Loss of cell identity drives human aging, two new papers argue](https://erictopol.substack.com/p/loss-of-cell-identity-drives-human) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Two new high-profile papers — one in Nature and one in Cell — propose that the loss of cell identity, driven by epigenetic drift, acts as both a driver and a consequence of human aging. Eric Topol highlighted the work in his Substack, framing it as a shift beyond the old dogma that aging is simply the accumulation of mutations and cellular damage. If loss of cell identity is truly a driver of aging rather than just a byproduct, it reframes aging as a potentially reversible epigenetic process and opens new avenues for intervention. This could reshape how researchers think about age-related diseases and the development of therapies targeting epigenetic regulation. The papers link epigenetic drift — the gradual deregulation of DNA methylation patterns across the genome — to increased cellular heterogeneity and loss of identity, particularly in aging stem cells and immune cells. Critics note that the work does not yet explain the Hayflick limit or why different species age at vastly different rates.

hackernews · bookofjoe · Oct 1, 20:02 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49926411)

**Background**: Epigenetic drift refers to the age-related accumulation of changes in DNA methylation and other epigenetic marks that alter gene expression without changing the underlying DNA sequence. The Hayflick limit, discovered by Leonard Hayflick in 1961, describes how normal human cells divide only 40 to 60 times before entering senescence, a finding that first linked cellular aging to organismal aging. The new papers build on these concepts by arguing that cells progressively lose their specialized identity as epigenetic regulation degrades over time.

## [AI Ataraxos Beats Top Stratego Player on a Budget](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/ai-finally-beat-the-best-stratego-player-in-history-and-did-it-on-a-budget/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A team from Carnegie Mellon, MIT, NYU, and Stanford built an AI called Ataraxos that beat Pim Niemeijer, arguably the best Stratego player ever, 15 games to one with four draws. It trained on just 16 GPUs for a few thousand dollars, playing roughly 34 times fewer games than DeepMind's DeepNash. Stratego is a classic imperfect-information game where players cannot see the opponent's pieces, making it far harder for AI than perfect-information games like chess or Go. Beating a top human with a fraction of the compute suggests more efficient reinforcement learning methods could make such AI far cheaper and more accessible. The algorithm learned far faster than DeepMind's DeepNash, which was introduced in 2022, and still ended up much stronger. The work is documented in a Nature paper and an arXiv preprint (2511.07312).

**Background**: Stratego is a two-player board game launched in 1946 in which each side secretly arranges 40 pieces with hidden ranks, and players only reveal a piece's identity when it battles. Because neither player can see the opponent's setup, it is an imperfect-information game, a category that also includes poker and many real-world decision problems. DeepMind's DeepNash showed in 2022 that model-free deep reinforcement learning combined with game theory could reach expert level, but it required enormous training resources.

## [Ethereum Foundation launches zkAPI for private AI payments](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-10-01-ethereum-foundation-launches-zkapi-417504) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On October 1, 2026, the Ethereum Foundation and the Open Anonymity Project launched zkAPI on Ethereum Mainnet, a system that lets users prepay in ETH or USDC and query AI models or other metered APIs without revealing their identity. Users deposit funds into an on-chain vault and authorize spending with zero-knowledge proofs, so the API provider and payment server cannot link requests to a payer. This is a significant step toward separating payment from identity for AI services, addressing a real privacy concern for users of metered APIs. The Ethereum Foundation's involvement adds credibility, and the design could influence how privacy-preserving payments are built across blockchain and AI ecosystems. zkAPI uses Groth16 proofs over the BN254 curve and runs on Ethereum Mainnet today; users deposit ETH or USDC into a vault contract, pay only for what they use, and can withdraw remaining balances on-chain. The design was first proposed in February, and the system was built by Open Anonymity in collaboration with the Ethereum Foundation.

rss · The Block · Oct 2, 00:22

**Background**: Zero-knowledge proofs are cryptographic methods that let one party prove a statement is true without revealing the underlying information, and they are increasingly used to improve blockchain privacy. zkAPI applies this to API payments: instead of linking a credit card or account to each request, a user proves they have sufficient prepaid credits without disclosing which credits are theirs. This matters because AI API providers typically see both who is asking and what is asked, creating a privacy risk that zkAPI aims to eliminate.
