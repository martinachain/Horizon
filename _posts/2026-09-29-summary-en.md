He stated that Hegota will be Ethereum's last 'normal' fork, and everything after it will be built on these cryptographic techniques. This proposal signals a major strategic shift for Ethereum, the largest smart contract platform, toward a zero-knowledge-centric architecture that could dramatically improve scalability and security. If realized, it would reshape Ethereum's development roadmap and influence how other blockchains approach proving and verification. Recursive STARKs allow proofs to verify other proofs, keeping verification costs flat even as computation scales, while formal verification uses mathematical methods to prove smart contracts behave as intended. Hegota, expected in late 2026, is set to introduce Verkle Trees and FOCIL (fork-choice enforced inclusion lists) before the new paradigm takes over.

**Background**: STARKs (Scalable Transparent ARguments of Knowledge) are a type of cryptographic proof that can prove a computation was done correctly without revealing the underlying data, and recursive versions let many proofs be compressed into one. Formal verification is a technique that uses mathematical proofs to guarantee code matches its specification, already used in Ethereum smart contract security. Ethereum's roadmap has traditionally advanced through hard forks, and Hegota is expected to follow Glamsterdam as the second major fork of 2026.
