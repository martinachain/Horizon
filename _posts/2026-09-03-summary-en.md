This anomaly could hint at new physics beyond the Standard Model, potentially involving dark matter or other exotic particles. While not a confirmed discovery, it highlights the sensitivity of current detectors and may guide future research in particle physics and cosmology. The LZ detector is located 1,480 meters underground in the Sanford Underground Research Facility in a former gold mine in South Dakota. It uses 7 tonnes of liquid xenon to search for weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs). The event is a 3-sigma anomaly, which historically has often disappeared with more data.

Direct detection experiments like LZ search for rare collisions between dark matter particles and atomic nuclei in ultra-sensitive detectors placed deep underground to shield from cosmic rays. The LZ experiment is a collaboration of 30 institutes from the US, UK, Portugal, and South Korea, and is currently accruing data towards a total 1,000 live day exposure.
