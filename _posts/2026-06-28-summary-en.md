## [Dan Luu Analyzes Artificial Discontinuities in Systems](https://danluu.com/discontinuities/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Dan Luu's 2020 article examines how artificial discontinuities in systems like tax brackets, marathon pacing, and test scores create perverse incentives and statistical artifacts. This analysis is significant because it reveals how seemingly arbitrary thresholds can distort behavior and data, affecting policy design, performance metrics, and decision-making across multiple domains. The article covers examples such as marathon finish times clustering around round numbers due to pace groups, and Polish language test scores showing a spike at 100 due to truncation, illustrating how discontinuities create misleading patterns.

hackernews · tosh · Jun 27, 13:32 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48698151)

**Background**: Artificial discontinuities are abrupt changes in a system's output that are not inherent to the underlying process but are introduced by design, such as tax brackets or grading cutoffs. These can lead to perverse incentives where individuals optimize for the threshold rather than the intended goal, and create statistical artifacts that obscure true distributions.
