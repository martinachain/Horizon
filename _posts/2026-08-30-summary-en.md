NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is scheduled to launch on August 30, 2026, aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket. This mission will enable broad astronomical surveys, potentially measuring light from a billion galaxies, and its fully open data policy will democratize access to space data, allowing anyone to analyze it. The telescope is based on a spy satellite design, which contributed to its cost and schedule efficiency. It will operate at the Sun-Earth L2 Lagrange point, similar to JWST.

This article provides a novel perspective on why bugs are missed, which is relevant to software engineering and quality assurance. Understanding bug blindness can help improve testing strategies and user experience design. The article distinguishes between two causes of bug blindness: overly-aligned mental models (where developers share the system's blind spots) and completely unaligned mental models (where users' expectations differ from the system's behavior). Dan Luu uses examples from search results and physical-world analogies to illustrate these concepts.

**Background**: Mental model alignment is a concept in UX design where interfaces are designed to match users' pre-existing expectations. Cognitive biases, such as the halo effect, can contribute to cognitive blindness, where individuals fail to notice errors due to systematic patterns of deviation. In software development, this can lead to bugs being overlooked because developers and users have mental models that align with the system's behavior, making it difficult to see beyond the expected.
