Under the program, customers pay about $55 per month for two Tesla Powerwalls and receive bill reductions based on how much stored power they share with the grid during outages and high-demand periods. This demonstrates that distributed home batteries, aggregated through software, can replace centralized fossil-fuel peaker plants at scale, offering a replicable model for other states and countries wrestling with grid reliability and decarbonization. It also shifts the economics of energy storage toward consumers and raises new questions about who pays for and benefits from grid infrastructure. The program supplies two Tesla Powerwalls for roughly $55 per month, with customer bill credits tied to how much capacity they make available to the grid; the VPP has already displaced two peaker plants, showing it handles both outages and fluctuating demand. **Background**: A virtual power plant (VPP) is a software-orchestrated aggregation of small-scale distributed energy resources — such as home batteries, rooftop solar, and electric vehicles — that together function like a single conventional power plant. VPPs let individually tiny resources pool their capacity to provide grid services such as peak shaving, frequency regulation, and backup power, often at 40–60% lower cost than building or running peaker plants. Peaker plants are expensive, rarely used fossil-fuel generators that fire up only during periods of highest electricity demand.

## [Delhi Slashes Electricity Losses from 50% to 5%](https://spectrum.ieee.org/delhi-electricity-loss) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Delhi has reduced its power distribution AT&C (aggregate technical and commercial) losses from around 50-52% in the early 2000s to roughly 5-6% today, according to an IEEE Spectrum report. The turnaround was achieved through privatization of distribution companies, infrastructure upgrades, smart metering, enforcement against theft, and AI/SCADA-based grid monitoring. This is one of the most dramatic grid efficiency improvements in the world, showing that even severely loss-making power systems in developing countries can be turned around with the right mix of policy and technology. It offers a replicable model for other Indian states and emerging economies struggling with high distribution losses, which drain utility finances and discourage investment. The reforms began around 2002 with the privatization of Delhi's distribution utilities and were sustained by smart meters, SCADA automation, and AI-driven loss detection. India's national AT&C losses still stood at 16.16% in FY25, so Delhi's ~5-6% figure is well below the national average.

hackernews · rbanffy · Sep 29, 12:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49892245)

**Background**: AT&C losses measure the gap between electricity fed into a distribution network and electricity actually billed and collected, combining both technical losses (heat in wires and transformers) and commercial losses (theft and non-payment). In many developing-country cities, these losses historically exceeded 40%, making utilities financially unviable. Smart grids use sensors, communication networks, and IT systems to monitor and control power flows in real time, enabling utilities to pinpoint losses and improve reliability.

## [RSA Attack Impersonates Hardware Vault Without Stealing Key](https://decrypt.co/379505/rsa-attack-without-stealing-key-what-it-means-crypto) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A research team led by UC San Diego, working with France's Institute for Research in Computer Science, demonstrated that they could impersonate a hardware security module (HSM) and forge arbitrary 1024-bit RSA signatures without ever extracting or factoring the device's private key. The attack was detailed in a paper submitted to the IACR Cryptology ePrint Archive on September 20. This finding challenges the long-held assumption that hardware-based key storage is inherently safe, since an attacker can now forge signatures even when the private key never leaves the tamper-resistant device. It raises concerns about the robustness of current encryption standards and could accelerate the shift toward more secure alternatives such as post-quantum cryptography. The attack works by sending multiple queries to the HSM and exploits unpadded signing in hardware modules, allowing arbitrary 1024-bit RSA signatures to be forged without extracting or factoring the private key. The technique specifically targets 1024-bit RSA, a key size already considered weak by modern standards.

rss · Decrypt · Sep 28, 21:16

**Background**: RSA is a public-key cryptosystem whose security rests on the difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers; the private key is what allows its owner to create signatures that others can verify with the public key. Hardware Security Modules (HSMs) are tamper-resistant devices that store private keys and perform signing on request, and they are widely used to protect cryptographic operations in finance, cloud key vaults, and certificate authorities. Trusted Platform Modules (TPMs) and cloud-based key vaults serve similar roles. Previous hardware-related RSA weaknesses, such as the ROCA vulnerability in Infineon's RSALib, affected key generation rather than the signing process itself.
