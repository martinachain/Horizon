## [Google Advances Practical Private AI with Homomorphic Encryption](https://blog.google/security/how-google-is-making-private-ai-practical-with-homomorphic-encryption/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Google announced progress in making homomorphic encryption practical for private AI, introducing HEIR, an open-source compiler toolchain that converts pre-trained AI models to operate on encrypted inputs. Despite this, the company acknowledges significant performance overheads remain. This development could enable privacy-preserving AI inference and training, allowing sensitive data to be processed without exposure, which is crucial for sectors like healthcare and finance. However, the high computational overhead currently limits commercial viability, making it more of a stepping stone than an immediate solution. HEIR (Homomorphic Encryption Intermediate Representation) is an open-source compiler toolchain that can convert pre-trained AI models to operate on encrypted data. Performance benchmarks indicate latency overheads ranging from 100x to 10,000x, making real-time inference impractical; current viability is limited to batch processing or high-value, non-interactive queries.

hackernews · u1hcw9nx · Aug 14, 15:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49300314)

**Background**: Homomorphic encryption (HE) allows computations to be performed on encrypted data without decrypting it, preserving privacy during processing. However, HE has historically been computationally expensive due to large ciphertext sizes, bootstrapping overhead, and limited support for non-linear functions, hindering its practical use in AI. Google's HEIR aims to address these challenges by providing a compiler toolchain to optimize HE-based AI models.
