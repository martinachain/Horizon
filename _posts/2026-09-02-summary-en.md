## [Dropbox Breach via Lenovo ID Authentication Flaw](https://decrypt.co/377099/dropbox-security-breach) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Dropbox accounts were compromised when attackers registered Lenovo IDs using victims' email addresses, enabling passwordless sign-in to existing Dropbox accounts. The flaw has been fixed and all sessions previously authenticated through a Lenovo ID were expired. This breach highlights a critical vulnerability class in federated authentication, where relying parties trust email claims without proper verification. It affects Dropbox users, especially those without two-factor authentication, and underscores the need for robust identity verification in third-party ID integrations. The attack exposed approximately 5,000 accounts during August 2026, impacting users without two-factor authentication. The flaw is similar to CVE-2026-55075 (Coder) and CVE-2026-14781 (Keycloak), where email claims are used as account-joining keys across federated trust boundaries.

rss · Decrypt · Sep 1, 20:01

**Background**: Federated authentication allows users to sign in to third-party services using credentials from an identity provider, such as Lenovo ID. In this case, Dropbox trusted the email address from Lenovo without verifying that the user actually owned that email, allowing attackers to create a Lenovo ID with a victim's email and then access the victim's Dropbox account. This incident underscores the importance of verifying email ownership and implementing additional security measures like two-factor authentication.

## [SEC Modernizes Transfer Agent Rules for Blockchain Era](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-09-01-sec-seeks-update-1970s-era-transfer-agent-rules-for-blockchain-age-413278) ⭐️ 6.0/10

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has announced plans to update its transfer agent rules, which date back to the 1970s, to accommodate blockchain and tokenization technologies. This regulatory initiative aims to align legacy securities infrastructure with modern digital asset practices. This move is significant because it signals regulatory recognition of blockchain's role in securities settlement and could pave the way for broader adoption of tokenized securities. It affects transfer agents, issuers, and investors by potentially streamlining processes and reducing costs, while also clarifying compliance requirements for blockchain-based systems. The SEC's proposal is still in early stages, with details yet to be finalized. The update would likely address how transfer agents can use distributed ledger technology to record and transfer ownership, but must balance innovation with investor protection and market integrity.

rss · The Block · Sep 1, 17:16

**Background**: Transfer agents are entities that maintain records of securities owners and handle transactions like buying, selling, and transferring shares. The current rules were established in the 1970s, before the advent of blockchain. Blockchain technology offers a decentralized and transparent ledger that could automate and secure these processes, but integrating it into the regulated securities market requires careful regulatory updates.
