## [Researchers Steal Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs](https://stolen-thoughts.com/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new technique has been demonstrated that can 'steal' hidden reasoning traces from proprietary LLM APIs by replaying them into weaker, more easily jailbroken models. This method was published on a website called stolen-thoughts.com and has gained significant attention in the AI community. This is significant because it exposes a potential security and transparency flaw in proprietary LLM APIs, allowing users to access reasoning processes that providers intended to keep hidden. It could impact AI transparency, competitive advantage, and the ethical debate around model output usage. The technique involves taking a reasoning trace from a frontier model and replaying it into a weaker sibling model, which is then jailbroken to reveal the trace. The method reportedly works even when the API summary does not preserve the distinction between the answer and the derivation, as seen in some AIME problems with Opus 4.8.

hackernews · quantumgarbage · Aug 11, 13:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49257876)

**Background**: Reasoning models, also known as large reasoning models (LRMs), are LLMs fine-tuned to perform multi-step problem-solving by generating intermediate steps called reasoning traces. Proprietary LLM APIs often hide these traces to protect intellectual property and prevent model extraction attacks, where adversaries query the API to build a surrogate model. This technique highlights the ongoing cat-and-mouse game between model providers and those seeking to extract hidden information.
