## [NVIDIA's 45°C Cooling Cuts Data Center Water Use to Near Zero](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/liquid-cooling-ai-factories/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NVIDIA announced a liquid cooling architecture that allows coolant to run at 45°C, eliminating the need for energy-intensive chillers and reducing water consumption to near zero in favorable climates. This innovation addresses the growing water and energy crisis in data centers, which are critical for AI workloads, and opens up opportunities for waste heat reuse in district heating systems, potentially reducing operational costs and environmental impact. The coolant is a mixture of 75% water and 25% propylene glycol, entering processors at 45°C and exiting at about 55°C. Raising chiller plant temperatures by just 1°C cuts cooling energy costs by roughly 4%, and cooling can account for up to 40% of a data center's total electricity use.

**Background**: Traditional data centers use air cooling or liquid cooling with chillers to keep coolant at 21–30°C, consuming large amounts of electricity and water. NVIDIA's approach allows higher coolant temperatures, reducing or eliminating the need for chillers and evaporative cooling towers, which are the main water consumers.
