## [Supreme Court Rules Geofence Warrants Need Constitutional Protections](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jun/29/supreme-court-geofence-warrants-case-decision) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The US Supreme Court ruled that geofence warrants, which compel tech companies to provide location data of all devices in a specific area, must comply with Fourth Amendment protections, including probable cause and particularity requirements. This landmark decision sets a significant precedent for digital privacy, limiting law enforcement's ability to conduct mass, warrantless location surveillance and potentially affecting similar practices like automated license plate readers. The case involved Google's Sensorvault, which stores historical geolocation data; the court held that searching millions of users' data without individualized suspicion violates the Fourth Amendment.

**Background**: A geofence warrant is a search warrant that requires a company like Google to identify all mobile devices within a virtual perimeter (geofence) during a specific time. The Fourth Amendment protects against unreasonable searches and seizures, but its application to digital data has been evolving, with prior cases like Carpenter v. United States establishing that accessing historical cell-site location data requires a warrant.
