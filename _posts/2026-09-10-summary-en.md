## [Sebastian Raschka: GPT-6 Astra's 'Looped Transformers' Are Not a Novel Secret](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Sebastian Raschka published an analysis arguing that the 'looped transformers' or 'recurrent depth' technique reported in GPT-6 Astra is not a novel secret method, but a known approach equivalent to stacking more transformer layers while reusing weights to save GPU memory. He also discusses how this relates to hidden reasoning and chain-of-thought monitoring. The analysis counters media hype that framed looped transformers as a scary secret technique that undermines chain-of-thought monitoring, helping researchers and practitioners understand that the approach is a known architectural trade-off rather than a fundamentally new capability. It matters for AI safety discussions because it clarifies what actually changes—and what doesn't—for monitoring model reasoning. Looped transformers reuse the same weights across multiple iterations, which is functionally similar to stacking more layers but with lower memory cost; the technique has prior academic roots in universal transformers and work by researchers such as Will Merrill. The article notes that if a model's reasoning trace is fed back into the model instead of being output, that reasoning becomes hidden by definition, though it may still be extractable.

**Background**: Looped transformers, also called recurrent-depth or universal transformers, apply the same transformer block repeatedly to the input, allowing more computation without adding parameters. Chain-of-thought (CoT) monitoring is an AI safety approach that inspects a model's explicit reasoning steps to detect intent to misbehave, and it is considered a fragile opportunity because optimization could make the CoT less faithful to the model's actual decision process. GPT-6 Astra is a reported upcoming model whose architecture details have circulated in media, prompting technical scrutiny.
