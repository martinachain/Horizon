The technology uses a new NanoStack architecture that stacks two complete transistors (NFET and PFET) vertically using nanosheet building blocks, joined through thin dielectric bonding. IBM no longer operates its own fabs, having paid GlobalFoundries to take them over in 2014, so this technology is a research prototype intended for licensing to manufacturing partners.

The approach aims to leverage physical computing for potential energy efficiency gains. This work explores a fundamentally different computing paradigm for AI, which could lead to more energy-efficient hardware if scaled. However, the current simulation on conventional hardware does not yet demonstrate practical advantages. However, most modern AI relies on digital GPUs, and analog approaches face challenges in precision and scalability.
