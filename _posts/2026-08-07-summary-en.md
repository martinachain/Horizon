## [Inouye Telescope Observes Kelvin-Helmholtz Instability on Sun](https://nso.edu/press-release/nsf-inouye-solar-telescope-enables-major-discovery-of-a-hidden-solar-process/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Scientists using the NSF Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope have directly observed Kelvin-Helmholtz instability on the Sun's surface for the first time. This observation, published in Nature, provides direct evidence of a process long theorized to be crucial for solar heating and dynamics. This discovery is significant because it confirms a key mechanism for energy dissipation in the Sun, which is essential for understanding sunspot formation, solar flares, and the heating of the solar corona. It also demonstrates the unprecedented resolution of the Inouye telescope, opening new avenues for solar physics research. The Kelvin-Helmholtz instability occurs when there is velocity shear in a fluid, creating characteristic vortex-like structures. The observations were made at small scales (~100 km and below), which have been difficult to resolve until now. The Nature paper is open-access, allowing the scientific community to examine the details.

hackernews · neversaydie · Aug 5, 15:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49184355)

**Background**: The Kelvin-Helmholtz instability is a fundamental fluid instability that occurs when there is a velocity difference across the interface between two fluids or within a continuous fluid. It is visible in cloud formations on Earth, the Red Spot on Jupiter, and now on the Sun. The NSF Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope, a four-meter solar telescope on Maui, Hawaii, is the largest solar telescope in the world, capable of resolving features as small as 30 km on the Sun's surface.
